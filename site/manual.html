The controls

The Pocket Master has an encoder (turn and press) and two side buttons. A hold means 0.8 seconds. The side buttons act when they are let go, or once held for 0.8 seconds.

Powering on and off

Hold the encoder to switch the pedal on. The screen asks POWER ON? with NO and YES, NO first: turn to YES and press. NO, or no answer for 10 seconds, switches it off again. Letting go of the hold that switched it on answers nothing.

To switch off, open the menu, choose POWER and answer YES to POWER OFF?. The sound fades, the screen says OFF, and the pedal switches off. Connected to a computer by USB the pedal stays powered, its screen dark and silent; hold the encoder to start it again.

The molecule

Every sound is a molecule, drawn as a chemist draws one: the generator, a large yellow disc marked FM, bonded to one to five atoms, each an effect, in the colour of its family. Each atom takes the sound of the atom it is bonded to on the generator's side. The outermost atoms are heard, each where it stands across the screen, from left to right in the stereo field. Half of what you hear is the generator itself, in the middle; the other half is the atoms.

A ring lights round each disc while it sounds, and small dots travel along the bonds, larger when louder. A new molecule shows its generator's family for a moment.

  • Encoder press: a whole new molecule, generator and atoms, through a short fade.
  • Left button: the same atoms, with their values and LFOs, bonded anew (REWIRE). Some molecules can be bonded only one way, and say NO REWIRE. The sound crossfades to the new bonds.
  • Right button: every atom's values drawn anew (FX). The kinds and the bonds stay.
  • Left button held: new LFOs for the generator's four macros and for one value of each atom (LFO).

The generator

Two engines: six-operator FM and physical models. Each new molecule draws one of 66 families, a chord to play it on, and, for FM, four LFOs on its macros. A new molecule is never of the family of the last three, and often of another kind, so successive sounds contrast.

Ambient
Tonitor's families, drawn on its ambient path: METAL, DRONE, ITERATIF, SPECTRE, ABYSS, CHOIR, BOWED, HOLLOW, BLOOM, TIDE, WIND, PLUCK, MALLET, WARP, SHAPER, LADDER, REVERSE, CREEP, CLANG.
Harsh
Tonitor's harsher families, on its open path: BRUIT, FRICTION, SHARDS, STATIC, GLITCH.
Held
Recipes after Norilsk's: HUM (a machine room), SWARM (a buzzing mass), ORGAN (an organ in a ruin), MODES (metal in the wind), STEAM (a hissing pipe), SCRAPE, CORONA (a high shimmer), ASH (a dark drone under a dusty crackle).
Struck
Metal struck, never a drum, on clocks that never quite repeat: ANVIL, STAMP, RIVET and SHEET scatter their strikes in clusters and pauses; GEARS, PISTON, RATCHET and FORGE run like machines whose pace wanders, some with a hiss of steam between strikes.
From the FM literature
TRUAX (one set of partials, its energy moving: Truax's normal forms), HAMMER (Schottstaedt's piano), TAM (tam-tams and membranes), CHIME (Chowning's tubular bell, with its hum below the note), FORMANT (the chord fused in fixed resonances), GROWL (feedback through a chain of operators), GLOW (brightening as it dies), ALLURE (the timbre pulsing at 3 to 12 Hz).
Physical models
HARP (a plucked string), WIRE (a stiff struck wire, its partials stretched), SNARE (Karplus-Strong's drum), PIPE (a struck stopped pipe), SITAR (a buzzing bridge), GONG (stiff, its pitch falling as it dies), BOWL (banded waveguides: bars, glass, a Tibetan bowl), BAR (marimba, vibraphone, agogo, wood), BELL, SKIN (a membrane). Struck on the same clocks, or held by a dust of small strikes.

The atoms

Each atom is one effect, its colour its family: cyan for filters and formants, orange for drive and wave shaping, pink for frequency shifting and cross-modulation, green for resonators, violet for time and texture, pale blue for space. Every atom has four values, drawn within bounds where it is always heard.

FMFMThe generator: six-operator FM, the root of every molecule. FLFILTERA driven state-variable filter: low-pass, band-pass, high-pass or notch, resonant to the edge of screaming, never piercing. FOFOLDA wavefolder, with a second fold and a tone control. MUMULAWRate and bit reduction, the filter open enough to alias, driven into it. STSTRINGThree Karplus-Strong strings on the generator's root, ringing up to 8 s. VOVOWELThree formants morphing through vowels, driven. TATAPEEcho through a worn head: dropouts, saturation, age, hiss under the sound. RSREVERSEWindows of the sound played backwards, fed back. SPSPRINGA spring tank, with its chirps. GLGLITCHBent buffers: jumps and stutters through the last 0.74 s, never at another pitch. CLCLOUDLong grains under a slow cloud: pitched, dark, smeared. SCSCREAMA shaped feedback howl. RIRINGA diode ring modulator modulating itself. SVSTARVEStarved, gated fuzz (after the Fuzz Factory). CBCHEBA harmonic re-voicer using Chebyshev polynomials (after Airwindows' Pafnuty). BKBANKAn inharmonic resonator bank: bar, plate, bell, membrane (after Mutable Instruments' Rings). SMSMEARThe sound through twenty random taps over 12 to 46 ms: a grainy haze rather than a room. DFDIFFUSEFour allpasses in series (lengths from Mutable Instruments' Clouds): blurred edges, no tail of its own. SWSLEWLimits how far the sound can move in one sample: highs turned into dark, grinding ramps (after Airwindows' Slew). XOXORThe sound combined bit by bit (XOR, AND, OR) with itself a moment earlier: broken and digital, but following its shape. DSDUSTHiss and crackle where the sound moves, none where it is still (after Airwindows' TapeDust). WSWAVESETThe sound cut at its zero crossings into its own small cycles, replayed a group at a time: repeated, reversed, shuffled or left out (after Trevor Wishart). XMXMODThe sound against its own copy 1 to 40 ms back: folded, diode ring or a comparator (after Mutable Instruments' Warps). CSCHAOSA double-well oscillator pushed by the sound: light, a resonance at a fixed pitch; harder, rough; hardest, jumping between its wells. SRSPIRALA pitch shifter inside a feedback delay: every repeat transposed again, so echoes climb or fall in endless staircases. Its splice runs from smooth to the raw glitch of the Eventide H910. SOSOURCELPC, from IRCAM's source-filter tradition: the sound's body kept, its source replaced by noise (a whisper) or a buzz tuned to the generator's root; the body can be held. ENENVELThe sound's shape, not its colour: attacks taken away so plucks become swells (Schaeffer's cut bell), attacks sharpened, or the envelope inverted so decays come back up. LGLPGA Buchla low-pass gate struck by the sound's onsets, with a source of uncertainty drawing each strike's decay and colour. BABARBERA comb that rises or falls for ever: four sweeping taps faded in and out, a Shepard-Risset flanger; still in the middle of its rate. SKSTICKStick-slip friction: the sound drags a sprung mass that sticks and slips. Squeaks, creaks, bowed chatter, rubbed glass. MPMORPHOSpectral delay after Poullin's Morphophone (GRM, 1953): six bands, each delayed and fed back on its own, so attacks fan out into chirps. DIDISINTEGRATEShort read heads in series, pushed back by the sound's own level: smeared and granulated by how hard it is hit (after Airwindows). RDREEDThe sound blows a clarinet: a reed and a bore tuned about the generator's root (after the Synthesis ToolKit). Squawks, overblowing, multiphonics; it rests when the sound stops. NLNLFDobson's nonlinear filter: from a resonant filter into pitched buzz, squeals and chaos, its nonlinearity riding the sound's level. SUSUBHARMUndertones, as on the Mixtur-Trautonium: two squares dividing the sound's zero crossings by 2 to 8, or the sound ring-modulated by them. Erratic on noise. PKSPARKEvery rising zero crossing fires a spark, a falling damped burst part tone, part noise, at the sound's level (after Airwindows' Nikola): electric pulse trains locked to the sound. CTCONTRASTSpectral contrast after GRM Contrast: twelve bands, the strong raised (noise turns tonal) or the weak (tones turn to breath); a trace keeps only the loudest few. DODOPPLERA virtual source on an orbit: the pitch bends as it passes, the level and the air's darkness follow its distance (after GRM Doppler, IRCAM Spat). Motion

Hold the right button: the screen says MOTION, and the molecule starts to move by itself. Hold it again to stop it (STILL); the molecule stays as it is then. Motion is the molecule doing what molecules do.

Thermal motion
Every disc drifts on a path that turns smoothly. Bonds stretch and pull like springs towards their length, discs that come too close push each other away, and the screen's edges turn them back. Each atom's place across the screen is its place in the stereo field.
Rearrangements
At the patch's motion rate, one event, drawn at random:
  • rewired (two in eight): the same atoms bond anew, each where it stands; the new bonds draw them together over a second or two, and each atom's sound crossfades from its old source to its new one;
  • an atom grown (two in eight): a new atom appears beside the atom it bonds to and swells into the molecule, its sound coming in dry and turning wet;
  • an atom pruned (one in eight): an outermost atom shrinks away, its sound turning dry and leaving the mix, and then it goes (never the last atom);
  • new values (three in eight): one atom's values glide to new ones, over the whole time until the next event.
The generator
Never touched: it is the voice, and the molecule goes on searching around it.

Nothing jumps: nothing is faded out or restarted, and the sound is a continuum. Only the values that can move smoothly glide; switches, such as a filter's type, keep what they are. The drift's pace follows the motion rate too: faster at a higher rate.

A patch saved while moving keeps the molecule as it was at that moment. The encoder press still draws a new molecule, and motion goes on with it.

The menu

Hold the encoder on the molecule. One item at a time, its colour round the screen: SETTINGS, PATCHES, POWER. Turn for the next, press to choose, hold to go back. The molecule goes on sounding.

Settings

The patch's own settings, saved with it, one a page; the dots below say which. Turn to change the value; the arrows show which way it can go. A press goes to the next page (after the last, back to the molecule); a hold or a side button goes back. A new molecule keeps what you set; a loaded patch brings its own.

  • MOTION RATE: motion's events a minute, 6, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 67, 90 or 120 (20 to begin with).
  • MIX: how much of the sound is the effects, 0 to 100 % in steps of 5 (50 to begin with). At 0 only the generator is heard; at 100 only the atoms.

Patches

Twelve numbered slots, four a row. An empty slot is outlined; a filled one is solid white; the chosen one is ringed in yellow. Turn to choose a slot.

  • Press: loads the slot's patch (LOADED 6). An empty slot says EMPTY 6, and the slots stay open.
  • Hold: saves the molecule there as it sounds now: its shape, its atoms' values and LFOs, the generator's patch, chord and clock, and its motion rate (SAVED 6). A filled slot is written over.
  • A side button: back to the molecule.

Patches are kept in the pedal's flash and survive switching off and installing a newer Molecular.

Power

POWER OFF?, NO first. YES switches the pedal off; NO goes back to the molecule, still sounding.

Update mode

The install page puts the pedal in its update mode by itself. To do it by hand, hold both side buttons for 3 seconds while Molecular runs. The screen goes dark and the pedal waits for an install. Switching it off leaves update mode: the next start is Molecular again, unchanged.
